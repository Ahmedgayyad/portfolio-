IRYSS CRM

Period
Nov 2025 – Jan 2026
Type
B2B SaaS CRM Platform
Services
Product, Brand, Development

About

IRYSS CRM was designed for enterprise sales teams that manage large volumes of leads, contacts, companies, tasks, and customer interactions every day.

Outcome

The final product shifted how users interacted with the CRM: the layout focused on clear data visibility, enabling seamless workflow transitions without feeling overwhelmed.

Data tables became completely interactive, allowing quick inline edits, column sorting, filtering, and mass updates right from the main view.

By creating a customizable dashboard structure, team leads gained instant visibility into daily metrics, revenue forecasts, and sales velocity.

Problem

Before designing IRYSS CRM, enterprise sales teams struggled with fragmented data across multiple tools: contacts in spreadsheets, tasks in notes apps, and deals tracked in legacy software.

The real problem was not data storage. It was visibility and speed. Sales reps wasted hours clicking through sub-menus to update a single lead status.

When deal stages disappeared from view, sales reps defaulted to manual follow-up reminders on paper or missed critical opportunities altogether.

Most productivity and CRM tools helped organize information, but slowed down actual execution.

Research

Research revealed a pattern I did not expect: sales reps spent over 60% of their day inside data tables and lists, yet existing CRMs forced them to open individual modal dialogs for every small update.

Not because modals were more powerful, but because legacy platforms failed to provide inline table editing.

Several key patterns became clear during research:

  • Users spend most of their active work time inside data tables.
  • ~61% struggled to complete multi-click lead updates efficiently.
  • ~50% wanted to customize pipeline views without complex query builders.
  • ~50% required clear team activity feeds and deal velocity tracking.

Product System

I kept noticing the same pattern: consistency reduces cognitive load.

Enterprise platforms already work best when interaction patterns are predictable. A column for status, a drawer for details, a table for overview.

That consistency became the foundation of the experience. People do not need to relearn how the product works when moving between different management views.

I enforced strict constraints throughout the project:

  • Reusable tables across every management module.
  • Consistent filters and saved views.
  • Shared modal forms and inline editing patterns.
  • Subtle feedback reinforcing progress and completion.

Role

I was responsible for designing the product from the ground up, turning complex business workflows into a scalable CRM experience.

Throughout the project, I worked across every stage of the design process:

  • UX research & competitive analysis
  • User flows & information architecture
  • UI design & design system
  • Interactive prototyping
  • Developer handoff & design QA

Learnings

1. Product quality comes from reducing friction repeatedly.

I used to believe product quality came from big bold features. I learned that most quality comes from fixing small sources of friction that add up over time.

2. Consistency reduces complexity.

Instead of introducing new layouts and interaction patterns for every feature, I relied on a consistent system of navigation, tables, forms, filters, and actions that users could recognize throughout the product.

3. Small interactions have a big impact.

Micro-interactions, optimistic UI updates, and instant visual feedback turn complex data entry into a fast, satisfying user experience.

4. A design system is a product investment.

Investing early in modular UI components paid off by allowing rapid iteration across new CRM modules without creating design debt.
